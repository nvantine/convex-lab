What Pareto optimal means

A solution is Pareto optimal if improving one criterion requires worsening another. Each arrow indicates the desired direction. Shading in two dimensions marks outcomes dominated by a displayed point. Dotted segments interpolate between samples; points on those segments have not been solved.

A strictly positive weighted sum produces a Pareto optimum when solved globally. For convex problems, supporting nonnegative weights can recover every Pareto point; zero weights may leave ties, so we refine them with a positive secondary objective. Nonconvex fronts can have unsupported points that weighted sums miss. A finite grid only samples the frontier.

Shown points pass solver and constraint checks and are nondominated among the computed candidates at a normalized tolerance of {{ experiment.result.dominance_tolerance }}. This is numerical evidence, not a proof of the complete frontier.
